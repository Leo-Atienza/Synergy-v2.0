# 05 · Hard questions (the finalist drill)

> Sanctuary finale prep. Front door: [`README.md`](README.md). The broad everyday set is in [`04-judge-faq.md`](04-judge-faq.md). These are the sharp, adversarial questions a strong finalist judge asks. Drill them out loud until the answers are reflex.

**Format:** **Say** is the spoken answer. *If pressed* is the follow-up. *Guardrail* is the trap to avoid. Most of these have no clean win, only an honest one. The honest answer is the winning answer at this event.

---

## The reframe that wins question 1 (read first)

The most common opening attack is some version of "Peel doesn't run cooling centres, so why not advocate for official ones?" or "aren't you just labelling ordinary buildings?" Both are answered by the same reframe:

> **We are not building new things. We are using buildings people already know.**
>
> "The point of Sanctuary isn't to build new cooling centres. It's to identify buildings people already go to, already trust, and can already reach. Harden a building people know, and they'll actually use it when it matters. A new facility nobody recognizes doesn't solve the problem."

This is the 20 percent trust factor in plain language, and the Brampton Lighthouse Project (79 places of worship, 39 signed agreements) proves the trusted-building model works in Peel.

---

## A. Problem and relevance

**Q1. Peel doesn't run designated cooling centres. Why target these five buildings instead of advocating for official city-run centres?**
> **Say:** "Because advocacy is slow, and these buildings already exist and are already trusted. We're not arguing against official centres. We're saying harden the buildings people already walk into. A new official centre nobody recognizes won't get used in a crisis; a hardened library people already visit will. That's the 20 percent trust factor in our score, and the Brampton Lighthouse Project shows the trusted-building model already works here."
- *Guardrail:* Don't criticize the Region. Frame Sanctuary as a complement, not a replacement.

**Q2. What does "hardening" actually prevent, deaths, ER visits, grid stress? How do you quantify the benefit without measured impact?**
> **Say:** "We deliberately don't put a number on lives saved. That would be the overclaim we avoid. What hardening delivers is concrete: a cool, powered place that stays open when the grid goes down, with backup power for cooling, charging, and medication refrigeration. The honest benefit is readiness. The measured impact comes after a real deployment, and we don't fake it beforehand."
- *If pressed:* "Public-health research ties extreme heat to excess deaths and ER visits. We point at that risk; we don't claim to have measured our own effect on it."

**Q3. Why Peel specifically? What makes Peel's gap worse than Hamilton or Oshawa?**
> **Say:** "Three things line up in Peel. It publishes a tract-level Heat Vulnerability Index we can build on honestly, which not every region has. It does not run designated seasonal cooling centres, so the gap is real, not hypothetical. And it's about 1.5 million people, roughly half born outside Canada, where trusted community and faith buildings carry real weight. The method isn't Peel-only, but Peel is where the data and the gap meet."

**Q (new). Peel already publishes the HVI. A planner can already see the hot tracts. What does Sanctuary add?**
> **Say:** "The index tells you where it's hot. It doesn't tell you which building to do something about. Sanctuary joins the heat layer to specific, named, trusted buildings and ranks them, so a planner goes from 'this tract is vulnerable' to 'call Malton Community Centre and Library first.' We turn a risk map into a to-do list."

**Q (new). Is this really an energy project, or a public-health project wearing an energy hat for the sponsors?**
> **Say:** "It's both, and that's the whole idea of a resilience hub. The trigger is public health: heat and outages hurt vulnerable people. The intervention is energy: solar and a battery so the building keeps cooling and power when the grid fails. That's why it fits Alectra. A hardened hub is a distributed energy resource that helps the grid on a normal day and islands during an outage. The health need and the energy asset are the same building."

## B. The scoring model

**Q4. The 35/25/20/10/10 weights are your judgment, not a published formula. Did you test weight sensitivity?**
> **Say:** "We're upfront that the weights are our judgment, structured on Peel's own sub-scores and the USDN hub functions. We didn't run a formal sensitivity sweep in a weekend, and I won't pretend we did. But the top tier holds: the three quintile-5 buildings lead on the one fully-verified factor, heat, so they stay on top under any reasonable weighting. A real deployment tunes the weights with the people who own the decision."
- *Guardrail:* Don't claim an analysis you didn't run. The honesty is the point.

**Q5. Vulnerable population (25 percent) is still pending. Why finalize the top five before verifying your second-heaviest factor?**
> **Say:** "Fair challenge, and it's why the rank is heat-led, not score-led. We don't let the modelled population proxy decide the five. Heat, the verified factor, leads. The population factor is a modelled 500 metre proxy, labelled as such, and the head count is pending for nine of ten. The five would be re-checked the moment a real walkshed lands. They aren't a verdict; they're where a planner looks first."

**Q (new). Why five buildings? Why not three, or twenty?**
> **Say:** "Five is a planning-budget framing, not a data threshold. 'If you can only harden a handful first, which?' forces prioritization, which is the point. The model ranks all ten and would rank more. Five is the number that makes a first capital round concrete. Change the budget and the cut line moves."

**Q (new). Three of your top five are in Malton. Aren't you over-concentrating on one neighbourhood?**
> **Say:** "Malton clusters because the heat risk clusters there. It's a genuine quintile-5 pocket, the index put it there, not us. Hardening the worst pocket first is the point, not a bug. The lower-ranked buildings in other parts of Peel are still in the dataset for the next round, and a real program would spread out after the first wave."

**Q (new). Your HVI is built on 2021 census data. Isn't your ranking already stale?**
> **Say:** "The 2021 census is the most recent national one, so it's the current best public source, not stale by choice. Tract-level heat vulnerability moves slowly; the urban heat island and building stock don't shift year to year. When the next census lands, the same pipeline re-runs on it in a day. We're current as of the best data, and we date every layer."

**Q (new). Peel includes Caledon, but your top five are all Mississauga and Brampton. Did you ignore Caledon?**
> **Say:** "Caledon is in the facility data, but it's rural and lower on the heat index. The urban heat island is a Mississauga-and-Brampton phenomenon, and the index reflects that. Caledon's resilience need leans more on winter and outages than urban heat, which is exactly what our winter and flood layers start to address. It ranks where the heat data puts it, it isn't ignored."

## C. The five buildings and access

**Q6. Sri Guru Singh Sabha ranks number two. How do you handle different opening hours, governance, and public-access obligations versus a city-owned centre?**
> **Say:** "Those are exactly the pending, site-audit questions we flag, not things we assume. A faith building's hours, governance, and willingness to serve the broader public in an emergency all have to be confirmed with the owner before it's a real hub. What the ranking says is narrower and honest: it's a trusted, high-heat-risk building worth talking to first. The Lighthouse Project shows faith institutions signing exactly these agreements, so it's realistic, but it's a conversation, not an assumption."
- *Guardrail:* Never imply the gurdwara has agreed to anything.

**Q7. Can someone actually reach Malton in 500 metres on foot from the highest-risk tracts, or do highways, rail, and missing sidewalks break that assumption?**
> **Say:** "You've found our biggest honest limitation. A 500 metre circle is straight-line, not a real walk. Highways, rail, and missing sidewalks absolutely break it, and Malton sits near both an airport and rail corridors. That's why the catchment is labelled modelled and the count is pending. Replacing the circle with a real walkshed that accounts for barriers and transit is step one of the roadmap. We'd rather show the circle and label it than pretend it's a walk."

**Q (new). What if a site audit shows your number one, Malton, can't take solar, bad roof, no electrical room? Does the whole demo fall apart?**
> **Say:** "No, because we never claimed it could. The roof class is modelled and the readiness is pending. If Malton fails its audit, that's the system working: the screen sent the auditor to the right place, and number two moves up. The ranking is which buildings to investigate, and 'investigate' includes the answer 'no.' A tool that can't be wrong isn't a planning tool."

## D. The honesty framework

**Q8. What does it take for a "pending" item to become "verified"? Who does the audit, and what does it cost?**
> **Say:** "A site visit by the building's operator plus an electrical and mechanical assessment. Verified means someone physically confirmed backup power, electrical capacity, roof condition, cooling, and accessibility, and the owner agreed to emergency use. That's professional engineering time per building, a municipal or utility cost, not something a hackathon team can do from a desk. We map who should do it; we don't claim we did."
- *Guardrail:* No dollar figure. "Professional engineering time" is the honest level of detail.

**Q9. None of the five buildings have confirmed backup power. Aren't they just ordinary public buildings with a good heat score?**
> **Say:** "Today, yes, and we say so on every card: candidate hub, not equipped. That's the honest starting point, not something we hide. The value isn't claiming they're ready. It's answering which ordinary-but-trusted buildings to invest in first, so they stop being ordinary. The backup power is the proposal. The ranking is which five to propose it for."

**Q (new). If the one battery at a hub fails during the event you built it for, haven't you given people false confidence?**
> **Say:** "Which is why readiness and maintenance are pending, audited items, not assumptions, and why a resident-facing layer only launches with live status. A hub that's down should show as down. False confidence comes from claiming readiness we haven't verified, which is the exact thing our labels exist to prevent."

## E. Feasibility and stakeholders

**Q10. Which of the three desks, Peel Climate, Emergency Management, Alectra, actually has budget authority? Who's the real decision-maker?**
> **Say:** "For capital, the Region of Peel's Office of Climate Change and Energy Management, working with council, holds the resilience-investment lever. Emergency Management owns activation, opening the buildings, not the capital. Alectra brings the energy side and can co-fund the solar-and-battery piece as a distributed energy resource. So the realistic first call is the Region's climate office, with Alectra as the energy partner."
- *Guardrail:* Say "realistically." We're reasoning about who holds the lever, not quoting their budget.

**Q11. Walk through the 15 percent Clean Electricity credit in practice. Who applies, what's the capital cost per building, is the grant enough?**
> **Say:** "The building owner, a municipality or a tax-exempt organization, claims the 15 percent credit as a refundable credit on the solar and storage equipment. It's the one federal credit tax-exempt owners can reach; the 30 percent Clean Technology credit is taxable corporations only. On its own 15 percent isn't enough, which is why we stack it with the Green Municipal Fund and Save on Energy. We deliberately don't quote a cost per building. That's a post-audit engineering number, and quoting it now would be the false precision we avoid."
- *Guardrail:* No dollar figures. "Not alone, it stacks" is the honest answer on sufficiency.

**Q12. Has anyone from Peel Region, Mississauga, or Brampton seen this? What was their reaction?**
> **Say:** "Not yet, and I won't pretend otherwise. This is a weekend prototype built on their public data. The honest next step is exactly that conversation: take the top five to the Region's climate office and Alectra. What we can say is that every input is their own public data, so there's nothing to surprise them, and the framing matches how the Region already talks about heat vulnerability."
- *Guardrail:* Never claim endorsement you don't have.

**Q (new). What stops a consulting firm, or the Region itself, from doing this with a GIS analyst in a week?**
> **Say:** "Nothing, and that's almost the pitch: it's simple enough to be real. What we add is the specific framing, trusted buildings as candidate energy hubs, the honesty discipline that keeps it defensible, and a working, reproducible pipeline they can run today instead of scoping a project. We'd be glad if the Region took it and ran it. The method is meant to be adopted."

**Q (new). Who keeps this updated after the hackathon? Open-source, abandoned, or a product?**
> **Say:** "Honest answer: today it's a prototype, and its future depends on a partner picking it up, the Region or Alectra. The pipeline is documented and reproducible by design, so it doesn't depend on us. We'd rather hand a working, auditable method to the people with the mandate than pretend we'll operate it ourselves."

## F. Scalability

**Q13. Does the HVI methodology transfer to Vaughan or Hamilton, or do you need a different vulnerability index per municipality?**
> **Say:** "The method transfers; the layer swaps. Peel publishes its own Heat Vulnerability Index, and many Ontario public-health units publish comparable ones, or you build one from the same census inputs: exposure, sensitivity, adaptive capacity. In Hamilton you'd drop in Hamilton's heat layer and its trusted buildings and run the same five-factor score with the same honesty labels. The pipeline is the product; the data is local."

**Q14. The resident-facing map needs real-time data, owner agreements, and liability coverage. What's the realistic timeline?**
> **Say:** "Years, not months, and on purpose. We're explicit that the resident map only launches after sites are verified, owners have agreed, hours and live status are trustworthy, and emergency managers sign off on the wording. Pointing a vulnerable person at an unverified building is a liability we won't take. The planning layer is useful now; the public app waits until it's safe."

**Q15. The Brampton Lighthouse Project already has 39 signed faith-partner agreements. Why build a new scoring system instead of replicating that model directly?**
> **Say:** "We're not replacing Lighthouse, we're extending it. Lighthouse proves the trust-network model and already has the agreements. What it doesn't do is rank by heat risk or add the energy layer. Sanctuary answers 'of the trusted buildings, which sit in the hottest tracts and should get solar and battery first?' Lighthouse is the partner network; we're the prioritization and the energy hardening on top of it. The honest move is to build on their 39 agreements, not around them."

## G. Technical and design

**Q16. How did you decide the verified, modelled, pending labels needed to be in the UI, not just the methodology docs?**
> **Say:** "Because a methodology doc gets read once and forgotten, but a label at the point of the number can't be missed. The whole project lives or dies on not overclaiming, so the honesty had to be where the eye lands: on the panel, in the legend, next to every figure, not in an appendix. We treat the label as a first-class part of the interface, the same weight as the number it qualifies."

**Q17. How does Sanctuary operationalize "trust" beyond a building's official civic or faith designation?**
> **Say:** "Honestly, today it's a proxy. We use the building's public civic or faith role, backed by its own official page, as a stand-in for trust, and we tag it verified only in that narrow sense. Real trust, do people actually go there, do they feel safe, is the language right, comes from community engagement, not a map. That's a named limitation. The roadmap step is working with community organizations and the buildings themselves to confirm that trusted-on-paper means trusted in practice."
- *Guardrail:* Don't claim you measured trust. It's a designation proxy, and saying so is the credible answer.

**Q (new). You said the StoryMap is judged and the site is a backup. Which am I looking at right now, and is this the real thing?**
> **Say:** "What you're looking at is the live support site, real and interactive, every number on it traceable. The judged qualifier artifact was the StoryMap video, which demos the same decision. Same project, same data; the site just lets you click instead of watch. Nothing here is a mockup."

## How to drill this file

- Read each **Say** out loud once. If it takes more than two breaths, shorten it.
- For each question, the person who answers names the honesty label in the answer (verified, modelled, or pending).
- Assign owners: data and scoring questions to the verification lead, code questions to a programmer, feasibility and funding to research and pitch. Everyone must know question 1, question 9, and "are these already hubs."
- The judge wins a point only if you overclaim. Stay honest and there's no opening.
