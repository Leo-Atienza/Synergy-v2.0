# Sanctuary Judge Q&A

## Are these buildings already resilience hubs?

No. They are **candidate hubs**. Sanctuary ranks where hardening should be investigated first.

## Are the solar and battery numbers measured?

No. They are planning estimates from rough roof/hardening class and regional solar context. The prototype's real output is the siting and prioritization layer.

## Are the reachable population numbers exact?

Not unless the final StoryMap uses Network Analyst. The default hackathon version uses a modelled 500 m catchment and labels it as such.

## Why include places of worship?

Because resilience is not only about public ownership. In many communities, gurdwaras, mosques, mandirs, churches, and community centres already hold trust, volunteers, and local knowledge. Sanctuary treats that as community infrastructure.

## Is this tokenizing faith communities?

No. The candidate list mixes civic and faith/community buildings, and every site is framed as an asset. The pitch is not "vulnerable people need churches." The pitch is "trusted buildings are part of the resilience network."

## Why not just build more official cooling centres?

That may be part of the answer. Sanctuary helps decide where new or upgraded safe spaces would protect the most vulnerable residents first.

## Can Alectra actually use this?

Yes as a planning concept. Alectra and municipal partners can use this kind of layer to identify community-energy and resilience-hub candidates before deeper engineering and partner engagement.

## What would happen after the hackathon?

1. Verify the top five sites with owners/operators.
2. Replace 500 m buffers with real walksheds.
3. Run site audits for roof, electrical, cooling, and backup-power capacity.
4. Compare funding paths for solar, storage, and facility upgrades.
5. Repeat the scoring across more of Alectra's service territory.
